abstract: "Detecting anomalies in multivariate time series derived from chemical process variables is a critical approach to ensuring operational safety, as failures in detection may result in the cascading propagation of local anomalies, potentially leading to major accidents. In the catalytic cracking reaction regeneration process, complex coupling relationships exist among variables, making it difficult for traditional models to accurately capture the spatiotemporal dependencies inherent in such data. To address this challenge, a diagonal state space autoencoder (DS2AE) is proposed for anomaly detection. This model integrates diagonal state space structures with multi-layer perceptrons and residual connections to effectively extract both temporal and spatial dependencies from multivariate time series, while enabling parallel inference across all time steps. Furthermore, a ternary search algorithm is introduced to facilitate automatic threshold selection, enhancing the objectivity of the detection process. Experimental results on a dataset generated by a simulated catalytic cracking process demonstrate that the proposed model achieves an F1 score of 92.55 % in the binary classification task of anomaly detection. The model also exhibits strong robustness with respect to hyperparameter selection, indicating its potential applicability in real-world industrial settings."
